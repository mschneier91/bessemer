No benefit to the rotational form on this flow

  • Same step limit. With BDF2/EXT2 both schemes are accurate up to CFL 0.7 and both degrade at 0.9. The rotational scheme cannot take a larger step than IMEX and stay accurate.
  • Same work per step, a little more time. At equal CFL both take the same steps and the same solver iterations. The rotational step costs 7–9% more wall time.
  • Lift is comparable; drag is not. The rotational form's drag-peak error is 2.2e-4 at every step size, against 3–4e-6 for IMEX. It is a spatial error of the form.
  • BDF2/EXT3 fails silently at CFL 0.7 in both schemes. EXT2 is accurate there, so bessemer now defaults to BDF2/EXT2. The EXT3 runs are the open markers in the charts.
  • Bigger steps save less than the step count suggests. Going from CFL 0.5 to 0.7 cuts the steps by 29% but the wall time by only about 10–15%, because the velocity solver needs more iterations per step.

Mesh M1: 832 curved cells, about 19k unknowns, Q3/Q2, grad-div γ = hK. Both schemes use a Dirichlet outflow (the inflow profile), which moves the forces by about 1e-8 relative to John's do-nothing reference. Four MPI ranks per run, two runs at a time on separate physical cores. Write-up: docs/imex_vs_semi_implicit.md §7.
